Start with the risks, not the demo

An AI agent is not just a chatbot with a nicer prompt. Once it can call tools, send messages or hit a paid API, one bad prompt or one runaway loop can leak data or burn a budget. I mapped that to OWASP LLM10, Unbounded Consumption, when I built BleedStop.

If I were hiring, I would ask four things first:

  • How do you stop prompt injection from reaching a tool or a spend?
  • Where is the cost limit enforced, and what happens when it is hit?
  • How do you evaluate the agent, and what did the eval miss?
  • Can we actually work in the same hours? I am in PKT (UTC+5).

Security: what to check

Ask whether defence sits in front of the model, or whether the model is asked to be careful. In BleedStop, the default check is a phrase list. A DeBERTa classifier is a second stage I can turn on. On my held out set, that classifier caught 77.5% of injections at a 5% false positive rate. I wrote that up in the prompt injection defence post, including where the phrase list is weaker on the same set.

I would also ask: are provider secrets kept off the client? Is there an audit log of decisions, and can those rows be edited later? What happens when the defence stack itself fails? Fail open and fail closed are different products.

In BleedStop, every decision is written to a Postgres audit table. A trigger blocks UPDATE and DELETE, so a decision can be reviewed later and cannot be edited in place. The BleedStop build post walks through that design. I am not claiming this is a field study. The golden set and the rules share an author, so the accuracy numbers are not production results. Recall on that set is 48.0%. Precision is 100%, with no benign false positives. I would want a candidate to say the weak number too.

For a front-desk agent, the Agent Receptionist case study uses a different shape. Emergencies, opening hours and human handoff are routed by policy at zero tokens. Answers come from the business's own documents and carry citations. After the model, a per-sentence guard checks an entity allowlist, stated hours and required facts. Secrets and raw provider errors stay on the server. The two demo businesses, a hotel and a dental practice, are fictional. That is the honest scope.

Cost limits: what to check

Ask where the ceiling lives. A limit in a prompt is not a limit. A limit in one process will split if you put more than one instance behind a load balancer.

BleedStop is a control plane in front of LLM, SMS and payment APIs. An app calls POST /decide before it spends, and gets back ALLOW, LIMIT, CHALLENGE or DENY from deterministic per tenant rules. Redis holds the budget so several instances share one ceiling. On the case study, a correctness test of 2,000 concurrent calls against a $5 ceiling on 3 instances overspent by $0.000.

The measured numbers below were all taken by me on one shared 8 vCPU Linux machine. The load generator and the services share that hardware, so I treat the absolute rates as a lower bound for a real cluster.

Evals: what to check

Ask for the suite, the date, and the miss. A score without the miss is a marketing slide.

For Agent Receptionist, the live runs were on 24 Oct 2026, on those fictional hotel and dental businesses:

  • Deterministic eval 44/44 (hotel 23/23, dental 21/21).
  • Live Groq eval 43/44. One eval-regex miss: the check's regex was too narrow; the reply itself was right.
  • Live 20-question test 19/20, same cause.
  • Policy-routed replies use 0 tokens.
  • One live public-URL check was about $0.0002 per question, with first token around 840 ms. That is a single check, not a benchmark.

The source is private and available on request. I would improve the narrow eval regex, run larger live suites, and test on real business documents.

If you are hiring for voice or RAG work, I would also look at whether the person has fixed a framework, not only wrapped one. My public record is on the open source page: merged Pipecat #5760 (function call timeouts stayed armed across intermediate results, merged 15 Sep 2026), Genkit #6346 in the Python OpenAI plugin (merged 23 Sep 2026), and Strands Agents harness SDK #4313 (merged 14 Sep 2026). LiveKit Agents #7186 is still open. I checked it on 17 Nov 2026. Until it is merged, that is a proposal, not a change in LiveKit Agents.

UK mornings fall inside my working day. A US East Coast morning is evening for me. I am open to contracts and to full time remote roles.
