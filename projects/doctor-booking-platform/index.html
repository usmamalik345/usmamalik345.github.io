A doctor booking platform for clinics

The platform served about 50 doctors across 20 clinics and had paying customers. I have not published a public product name, revenue, a more precise customer count or clinic names, so this page uses the plain description.

The problem a clinic booking product has to solve

A clinic with more than one doctor, and more than one site, cannot run appointments only from a phone line. A patient needs to find a doctor, pick a time that is actually free, and get a reminder before the visit. Staff need to see the same diary when a booking changes, not a printout that is already wrong. Access has to be limited: a receptionist, a doctor and a patient should not share one unlocked account.

That is the problem space of a doctor booking platform. The scale I can state is the one already on this site: about 50 doctors across 20 clinics. I am not adding a bookings-per-day figure, a no-show rate, or a named clinic.

From March 2023 to June 2024 I was a full stack engineer at IT Pixelz. In that role I built the booking flows for this platform. The accurate wording is booking flows, not that I founded the company or that I was the only engineer on the product.

Other work in the same role, on other products, included Angular 17 features for an enterprise task management app, dashboards and reporting views in a CRM used to manage hundreds of client accounts, and Node.js and .NET microservices on Redis, MySQL and MongoDB, deployed to AWS with Docker and CI/CD. Those are separate from this booking platform. I mention them only so the role is not reduced to one product.

The stack, and what each part was for

The booking flows were built with React on the frontend and Node.js with Express on the backend, with MongoDB for data. That is a straightforward JavaScript stack for a web product that has to take a booking, store it, and show it again without a page refresh for every change.

  • JWT auth. Users signed in with tokens, so sessions could be checked on the API without sharing one open account across roles.
  • Live updates over WebSockets. When a booking changed, the people looking at that diary could see the update without waiting for a full reload.
  • Reminders. The system sent reminders for appointments, so a booking was not only a row in a database.
  • Gemini-assisted booking. Booking flows used Gemini to assist the booking step. I am not publishing prompts, a success rate, or a claim that the model ran the clinic on its own. It assisted booking, on top of the ordinary create-and-confirm path.

I have not published a hosting region, a database size, or a performance number for this product, so I will not invent them here.
